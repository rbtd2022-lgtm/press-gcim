November 26, 2026 Movement | Urgent Appeals

Comment by Global Citizens on claims for repayment of social assistance from Ukrainian citizens of retirement age in the Republic of Austria

The international movement Global Citizens has taken note of reports concerning the situation in the Republic of Austria involving claims against Ukrainian citizens of retirement age for repayment of social assistance previously granted to them.

According to Austrian media reports, overpayments made under the basic care system for displaced persons (Grundversorgung) are being recovered in the federal state of Lower Austria. Published reports put the total amount of the claims at no less than EUR 445,000.

The lawfulness of taking foreign pension payments into account when determining the amount of social assistance is not in dispute. Defining the parameters of social protection and the conditions governing public expenditure falls within the exclusive domestic jurisdiction of a sovereign state. At the same time, receipt of a Ukrainian pension cannot be regarded as evidence of deliberate concealment of income or abuse of social protection arrangements.

These public statements by representatives of the Lower Austrian authorities are a cause for concern. In remarks delivered on November 10, 2026, Deputy Governor of Lower Austria Udo Landbauer again referred to demands that Ukrainian citizens repay social assistance overpayments arising from pension income they had not declared. A similar position had previously been expressed by a representative of the Freedom Party of Austria in an interview with Kronen Zeitung. These statements indicate continued adherence to the previously stated approach to debt recovery, at a time when Ukrainian organizations had requested that the individual circumstances of assistance recipients be taken into account.

The International Movement Global Citizens also notes the emerging case law. In decisions dated September 28 and 30, 2026, the Lower Austria Regional Administrative Court confirmed that Ukrainian pension payments must be taken into account when determining the amount of government support. In one of the cases, the amount to be repaid was reduced from EUR 6,866.81 to EUR 5,936.81. In the other, the court upheld a repayment claim of EUR 7,536.11, notwithstanding the applicant’s arguments concerning her difficult financial circumstances and the absence of any intention to conceal income received.

In the latter case, while acknowledging the applicant’s limited financial means, the court pointed to the possibility of payment in installments or a deferral of payment. It nevertheless found no grounds for waiving reimbursement under Section 14(2) of the Lower Austria Basic Care Act. The protections provided by national law for socially vulnerable persons do not, therefore, lead in every case to relief from repayment obligations, even where immediate compliance would entail considerable financial hardship.

This raises the question whether existing safeguards adequately protect persons who, in objective terms, lack the resources to meet the claims against them. The possibility of repayment in installments does not eliminate the consequences of incurring the debt or ensure that the recipient’s financial position is restored.

The circumstances in which assistance was initially granted are also of material importance. According to published materials from Austrian organizations working to protect the rights of displaced persons, information about Ukrainian pensions was not always requested or taken into account at the initial application stage, including in cases where recipients themselves disclosed that information. Such circumstances require examination on an individual basis. Responsibility for shortcomings in the administration of assistance should not, without an appropriate legal basis, be placed solely on persons who sought state protection.

The situation of older persons who were compelled to leave Ukraine because of the armed conflict and who lack independent sources of income sufficient to meet their basic needs merits particular attention. Claims for repayment of substantial amounts covering earlier periods may give rise to obligations disproportionate to those persons’ actual financial means.

It is noted that Section 14(2) of the Lower Austria Basic Care Act provides for the possibility of waiving repayment, in whole or in part, where recovery would cause social hardship (soziale Härte), in particular by jeopardizing the means of subsistence of the person concerned. National law thus provides a legal basis for decisions that take account not only of the public budget but also of the recipients’ actual financial circumstances.

The International Movement Global Citizens takes the position that financial discipline must not be placed in opposition to the principles of proportionality, legal certainty, and respect for human dignity. Ensuring the lawful use of public funds entails not only identifying overpayments but also establishing how they arose and determining the respective responsibilities of those involved.

The International Movement Global Citizens draws attention to the inadmissibility of replacing an individualized legal assessment of the circumstances with generalized assumptions of bad faith concerning an entire category of assistance recipients. Delayed notification of income, possible shortcomings in administrative procedures, and deliberate submission of false information require separate assessment and cannot be treated as equivalent.

The International Movement Global Citizens calls upon the competent authorities of the Republic of Austria to ensure a thorough and objective examination of each recovery case, taking into account the circumstances of the initial award of assistance, the recipients’ actual financial position, their ability to meet repayment obligations, and the grounds provided under national law for waiving repayment in whole or in part.

The International Movement Global Citizens considers it appropriate to devote greater attention to protecting older persons for whom claims relating to debts accumulated over several years may result in a prolonged deterioration in living conditions. Recovery measures must be accompanied by appropriate procedural safeguards against disproportionate restrictions on the ability to meet basic needs.

It must be emphasized that upholding the law, protecting public finances, and safeguarding human dignity are not mutually exclusive objectives. Their consistent and balanced implementation constitutes an essential condition for a state governed by the rule of law and for responsible social policy.

Distribution: This comment is being transmitted through established channels to the Federal Ministry for European and International Affairs of the Republic of Austria and brought to the attention of the Embassy of Ukraine in the Republic of Austria.

For reference: The factual basis for this comment consists of reports published by the Austrian outlets Kronen Zeitung and Heute on October 20, 2026; an interview with a representative of the Freedom Party of Austria on Krone.tv on October 29, 2026; a report concerning remarks by Deputy Governor of Lower Austria Udo Landbauer on November 10, 2026; decisions of the Lower Austria Regional Administrative Court dated September 28, 2026, in case LVwG-AV-224/001-2026 and September 30, 2026, in case LVwG-AV-1515/001-2026; Sections 12 and 14 of the Lower Austria Basic Care Act (NÖ Grundversorgungsgesetz); and a joint position of Austrian organizations protecting the rights of displaced persons, published on October 20, 2026.

The figure of at least EUR 445,000 cited in those reports represents the reported preliminary total of claims and does not constitute an officially confirmed amount of funds actually recovered.
