October 28, 2026 Movement | Initiatives

Framework provisions
for the Global Citizens international initiative to advance scientific and institutional cooperation in health care in Ukraine

Initiating party: the international movement Global Citizens.

Recipient State: Ukraine.

Objectives of the initiative

This initiative is intended to help strengthen Ukraine’s standing in the international scientific and professional community. To that end, the initiative provides for expanded scientific cooperation, further development of medical diplomacy and multilateral engagement, and communication to international audiences about Ukraine’s achievements in medicine and pharmacy.

Establishment and operation of the Institute

Under the initiative, an independent nonprofit research institute is to be established in Ukraine, the Institute for Public Health, Medical Law and Policy (the Institute). Its legal and organizational form, internal structure, and governance arrangements are to be regulated in strict accordance with Ukrainian law, taking into account the Institute’s functions. It is noted that the international character of the cooperation does not predetermine the legal status of the Institute.

The Institute is expected to ensure that empirical research is conducted and, with the support of the Global Citizens movement, to develop international professional ties in health care, medicine, and pharmacy. Ukrainian and foreign specialists with relevant expertise are to be engaged in the Institute’s research and expert activities.

The Institute’s priorities

Under the initiating party’s proposal, the Institute’s principal tasks include presenting Ukraine’s scientific and professional capabilities to the international community, supporting continuing professional training for medical and pharmaceutical personnel, and preparing evidence-based recommendations for Ukraine’s state authorities. These efforts are intended to improve the legal and regulatory framework and strengthen arrangements for protecting the professional rights and legitimate interests of those personnel.

ZVYTIAHA specialized health care ecosystem project

The principal focus of the Institute’s applied research is to develop a concept for establishing ZVYTIAHA, a specialized health care ecosystem for veterans, drawing on leading practices in other countries.

The Global Citizens movement proposes that the ecosystem operate as an institutionally distinct health care system that integrates diagnostic and treatment processes, medical rehabilitation, the provision of medicines, and long-term clinical monitoring. Under the proposed system, care would be delivered in accordance with clinical protocols differentiated to address the specific needs of veterans.

The ZVYTIAHA concept must include measures to provide medical and legal support to veterans whose health was impaired during military service. These measures are intended to ensure continuity of treatment and rehabilitation, including during criminal proceedings.

Development of the concept is to include a comparative analysis of relevant approaches in the United States, the United Kingdom, France, Poland, and Hungary. Particular attention is to be given to the conditions under which continued treatment and rehabilitation through ZVYTIAHA could serve as a procedural alternative to deprivation of liberty, taking into account victims’ legal rights and public safety requirements.

The concept is intended to set out the ecosystem’s organizational and staffing structure and functions, arrangements for cooperation among the parties involved, criteria for developing and implementing clinical protocols for veterans, mechanisms for ensuring continuity of care, and the legal, financial, and economic conditions for establishing and operating ZVYTIAHA.

Groups to be served by ZVYTIAHA

As the concept is developed, the categories of veterans who need specialized treatment, medical rehabilitation, and long-term monitoring are to be defined in greater detail, taking into account the circumstances of their military service, their health, and their legal status under Ukrainian jurisdiction.

The legal grounds for granting veteran status in the United States and Ukraine are to be compared, and strict medical eligibility criteria for admission to the system are to be identified. By virtue of their active-duty status, service members are to be excluded from the group of potential recipients of specialized care through ZVYTIAHA.¹

Priority is given to ensuring coverage for individuals who sustained wounds, injuries, or lasting physical impairments or developed illnesses during military service, including in circumstances unrelated to direct participation in hostilities.

Drawing on international institutional experience

In line with the initiating party’s approach, the experience of the U.S. Veterans Health Administration (VHA) is considered the primary institutional reference for developing the ZVYTIAHA concept.

The comparative analysis is to focus on the regional organization of care through the Veterans Integrated Service Networks (VISN), on the operation of the Polytrauma System of Care and the Spinal Cord Injuries and Disorders System of Care, and on arrangements for continuous medical follow-up of veterans.

The study is also to examine the United Kingdom’s Op COURAGE and Op RESTORE initiatives, Croatia’s veterans center model (Veteranski centri), and relevant experience from other countries in addressing the health and social consequences of armed conflict. The extent to which these approaches are applicable will be assessed in light of the needs of Ukrainian veterans, applicable law, organizational and staffing conditions, and the resource capacity of Ukraine’s health care system.

Legal, regulatory, financial, and economic framework

Alongside the development of the concept, the Institute intends to prepare a set of proposals to update Ukrainian legislation to establish an institutional framework for the ZVYTIAHA ecosystem.

The legal analysis is to address safeguards for the professional activities of medical and pharmaceutical personnel and mechanisms for protecting the legitimate interests of pharmaceutical manufacturers and other parties involved.

The purpose of the financial and economic justification is to define arrangements for stable financing, parameters for preferential tax treatment, and a distinct package of government support measures. At this stage, the options to be considered include targeted subsidies for scientific research, incentives to develop and manufacture relevant medicines, support for investment, and modernization of health care infrastructure.

Resource mobilization

Financial and economic, material and technical, technological, and other resources are to be mobilized solely to put the ZVYTIAHA concept into effect.

Potential sources of donor support include international charities, foreign foundations, private investors, and foreign corporations, including business associations affiliated with the Ukrainian diaspora or maintaining stable trade and economic ties with Ukraine.

The economic justification is to specify the types and quantities of resources required and the arrangements for pooling them.

Expected outcomes

The initial phase of the initiative is to establish the institutional foundations for creating the Institute and subsequently expanding international scientific cooperation through it in health care, medical law, and pharmacy. This cooperation is intended to help strengthen Ukraine’s professional standing in relevant international associations.

Once the research cycle is complete, the Institute intends to develop scientifically grounded initiatives on the organization of health care, medical law, and sector-specific employment safeguards.

The principal practical outcome of this priority will be the development of a comprehensive concept for establishing the ZVYTIAHA ecosystem, setting out the rationale for organizational and staffing decisions and recommendations on the legal and financial support and resources needed to implement those decisions. The document must include a detailed analysis of whether extended treatment and rehabilitation can be used as a procedural alternative to measures involving deprivation of liberty. The concept is to be submitted to the parties involved for review so that they can agree on arrangements for its implementation.

Provided that specialized care and rehabilitation programs can be accessed without barriers in Ukraine, implementing the concept could reduce the incidence of Ukrainian veterans with substantial experience participating in armed conflicts having to travel to European or other foreign countries for specialized treatment.

Potential associated social and political effects include reducing the risk of difficulties with social adjustment and easing localized social tensions, both arising from unmet needs among veterans. Institutionalizing the ecosystem could also help prevent offenses and strengthen public safety in Ukraine.

These Framework Provisions were formulated following a closed expert roundtable held on October 22, 2026, on Preparing Ukraine’s Health Care System for the Transition from Wartime to Peacetime: International Experience in Post-Conflict Recovery, Medical Rehabilitation, and Institutional Reform.

Footnote ¹:

Under Ukrainian laws and regulations, in particular the Law “On the Status of War Veterans and Guarantees of Their Social Protection,” a distinction is drawn among participants in hostilities, persons with disabilities resulting from armed conflict, and participants in armed conflict. Military service alone does not automatically confer any of these statuses. Consideration must also be given to the circumstances of service in rear-area, medical, engineering, and other units and to injuries or illnesses sustained during service that subsequently require extended treatment. Determining which categories of people are to receive medical care through the proposed ecosystem is a separate task requiring comprehensive expert study and analysis.
