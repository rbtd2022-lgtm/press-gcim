Project profile
of the Global Citizens International Initiative to Develop Scientific and Institutional Cooperation in Ukraine’s Health Sector

Initiator: Global Citizens International Movement.

Country of implementation: Ukraine.

Purpose of the Initiative

To strengthen Ukraine’s international position and promote a positive image of the country within the international community through the development of scientific cooperation, science and medical diplomacy, and the presentation of the achievements and potential of Ukrainian medical and pharmaceutical science.

To this end, the Initiative envisages establishing the Institute for Public Health, Medical Law and Policy in Ukraine as an independent nongovernmental research institution (hereinafter, the Institute). Its work will combine research and expert activities with the development of international professional ties in health care, medicine, and pharmacy.

Principal Areas of the Institute’s Work

The Institute’s work will focus on presenting Ukraine’s scientific and professional potential internationally, advancing scientific cooperation and continuing professional development for medical and pharmaceutical professionals, and preparing and submitting proposals to Ukraine’s public authorities to improve legislation, public policy, and mechanisms for protecting the rights, freedoms, and legitimate interests of members of these professions.

Organizational Framework

Global Citizens is the initiator of the Institute’s creation and facilitates the establishment of the international scientific and expert ties required to implement the Initiative.

The Institute is to be established in accordance with Ukrainian legislation as an autonomous nongovernmental research institution. Its legal form, structure, and governance arrangements will be determined in light of its intended functions. The international nature of the cooperation does not, in itself, predetermine the Institute’s legal status.

The research and expert work is to involve relevant Ukrainian and international specialists.

Priority Area of Applied Research: ZVYTIAHA

The priority area of applied research will be the study of international experience and the development of a conceptual framework for establishing ZVYTIAHA, a specialized health care ecosystem for veterans.

Global Citizens envisages ZVYTIAHA as an institutionally distinct health care system that brings together diagnosis, treatment, medical rehabilitation, provision of medicines, and long-term medical follow-up within a single organizational model.

The conceptual framework is intended to define the system’s structure and functions, arrangements for stakeholder cooperation, approaches to applying relevant clinical standards, mechanisms to ensure continuity of care, and the legal and economic conditions for establishing and operating the ecosystem.

Target Group of the ZVYTIAHA Specialized Health Care Ecosystem

In light of the experience of the United States of America, particularly the Veterans Health Administration (VHA), in the course of developing the conceptual framework for ZVYTIAHA, it is envisaged that the categories of war veterans requiring specialized treatment, medical rehabilitation and long-term medical follow-up will be identified, taking account of the nature of their military service, their state of health and their legal status under the applicable legislation.

The definition of the target group will provide for the delimitation of the legal status of veterans in the United States and Ukraine, the medical criteria for admission to the ecosystem, and the exclusion of active-duty military personnel from the group of potential recipients of medical care.1

It is envisaged that particular attention will be given to persons who sustained wounds, injuries or disabling injuries, or fell ill during military service, including cases not related to direct participation in hostilities.

International Research References

The experience of VHA is regarded as the principal institutional benchmark for developing the conceptual framework for ZVYTIAHA.

The comparative analysis will cover the regional organization of medical care within Veterans Integrated Service Networks (VISNs), the specialized Polytrauma System of Care and Spinal Cord Injuries and Disorders System of Care, and arrangements for continuous medical follow-up for veterans.

The study will also consider the United Kingdom’s Op COURAGE and Op RESTORE programs, Croatia’s Veteranski centri model of veterans’ centers, and selected practices of other countries with experience in addressing the medical and social consequences of armed conflict.

The applicability of the approaches examined will be assessed in light of Ukrainian veterans’ needs, national legislation, organizational conditions, and the resources available to Ukraine’s health care system.

Legal and Economic Conditions for Implementing ZVYTIAHA

Development of the conceptual framework will be accompanied by proposals to improve Ukrainian legislation to enable the establishment and operation of the ecosystem.

The legal assessment will address guarantees of the professional activity of medical and pharmaceutical personnel within ZVYTIAHA and protection of the legitimate interests of pharmaceutical manufacturers and other participants in the system.

The economic assessment will provide for the development of financing mechanisms, preferential tax arrangements, and a distinct system of government support measures. As part of this assessment, consideration is to be given to targeted funding for scientific research, incentives for the development and production of specialized medicines, support for investment, and the development of health care infrastructure.

Resources

Financial, material, technical, technological, and other resources are to be mobilized exclusively for the practical implementation of the conceptual framework for the ZVYTIAHA specialized health care ecosystem.

Potential sources of support under consideration include international charitable organizations, foreign foundations, private donors, and foreign companies, including organizations established by members of the Ukrainian diaspora or maintaining sustained business and economic ties with Ukraine.

The resources required and the mechanisms for mobilizing them are to be determined as part of the economic assessment of the conceptual framework.

Expected Results

Implementation of the Initiative is intended to create the organizational framework for establishing the Institute and developing international scientific cooperation through it in health care, medical law, and pharmaceuticals, with a view to enhancing the prestige of Ukraine in the relevant professional communities.

The research will produce scientifically substantiated proposals concerning the organization of health care, medical law, and safeguards for the professional practice of medical and pharmaceutical personnel.

As part of the priority area of applied research, a conceptual framework for establishing the ZVYTIAHA specialized health care ecosystem for veterans is to be prepared, setting out the rationale for the proposed organizational arrangements and recommendations on the legal, economic, and resource arrangements needed to support them. The document is to be submitted to stakeholders for consideration and for the determination of the conditions for its subsequent implementation.

Implementation of the conceptual framework for ZVYTIAHA may help reduce the need for Ukrainian veterans with substantial experience in armed conflicts to seek specialized medical care and medical rehabilitation in European and other countries provided that the relevant services are available in Ukraine.

A reduction in the risks of social maladaptation and social tension associated with veterans’ unmet needs for treatment and medical rehabilitation is envisaged as a potential accompanying social outcome. In this respect, establishing the ecosystem may contribute to the prevention of offenses and to strengthening public safety in Ukraine.

Note ¹

The Law of Ukraine “On the Status of War Veterans and Guarantees of Their Social Protection” establishes an exhaustive list of categories of war veterans: participants in hostilities, persons with disabilities resulting from war, and war participants. Military service alone does not automatically confer that status. At the same time, military personnel may have served in rear, medical, engineering and other units, fallen ill or sustained injuries during their service, and subsequently required long-term treatment.

Determining which categories of persons should be eligible for care within the ecosystem is a separate question requiring comprehensive and in-depth study.
